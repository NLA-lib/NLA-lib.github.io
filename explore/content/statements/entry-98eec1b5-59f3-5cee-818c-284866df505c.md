::: theorem result:generalized-matrix-laplace name="The generalised matrix Laplace transform bound"
Let $\mu$ be any measure, and let $\mathbf{Y}$ take values in real or complex
$n\times n$ matrices with $n\ge1$, Hermitian $\mu$-almost everywhere.
Let $\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, and assume that
$\operatorname{tr}\psi(\mathbf{Y})$ is $\mu$-integrable. For
$\psi \ge 0$ nondecreasing on $[0, \infty)$, $t \ge 0$ and $\psi(t) > 0$,
$$\mu(\lambda_0(\mathbf{Y}) \ge t) \le
\frac{\int \operatorname{tr} \psi(\mathbf{Y}) \, d\mu}{\psi(t)}\,.$$
Here $\lambda_0(\mathbf{Y})$ is the largest eigenvalue and $\psi(\mathbf{Y})$
is defined by [functional calculus](/explore/statements/af61ef21-9f45-5d71-87c8-6d01c16be15e/).

Note: The cited bound is extended here to any measure $\mu$ (the proof is Markov's inequality,
`mul_meas_ge_le_integral_of_nonneg`, which needs no finiteness), over any `RCLike` field. No
continuity of $\psi$ is assumed: the spectrum of a Hermitian matrix is finite, so `cfc ψ` is never
its junk value at a Hermitian argument. `[Nonempty n]` is needed: for empty $n$ and $t = 0$ the
event is everything and the right side $0$. The integrability hypothesis `hint` excludes the junk
value $0$ of the integral.
:::
